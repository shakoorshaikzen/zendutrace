# Design System: XenTag Landing

## Overview

**Creative North Star: "The Broadsheet Spec Sheet"**

Operator-grade editorial: a $10 paper label sold like field equipment. The page reads as a broadsheet spec sheet — a bone-paper ground (#F5F2EA) whose every content section is framed by two vertical hairline rails, the way a broadsheet's column rules turn white space into a set measure. Live-platform moments (the walkthrough console, the integrations manifest, the CTA, the footer) sit on warm charcoal plates (#151210–#231D18) that carry no rails because they are their own plates. Cargo Ember (#BC3E10) is the only loud voice on the page: CTAs, live routes, active states, and exactly three underlined payoff words. Everything else argues with numbers, hairlines, and real footage.

The type does the selling, and it works **both ends** of Archivo's width axis. The display voice stretches wider as it grows (h1 at 125% width, h2 at 110%, h3 at 100%) — the extended-grotesque of freight stencils and equipment plates. Against it, the same face **condensed** (82–92%) sets every large figure and inline machine label, and that extended-against-condensed pairing is the type contrast the page is built on. IBM Plex Sans carries every human-written sentence: a technical-documentation face whose 516 x-height sits correctly against Archivo's 526, so body copy reads present rather than pale and weight can mean something again. JetBrains Mono is reserved for values that must **align in a column** — serials, temperatures, citations, API calls. Controls are squared and chunky at 6px radius — equipment buttons, not pills. Illustration is a single isometric hairline-dieline language: wireframe cartons, pallets, and radio arcs drawn in 1.1–1.6px strokes, never filled cartoons.

This world explicitly refuses the neon-dark AI-SaaS grammar: no two-tone gradient headlines, no glassmorphic decoration, no uppercase eyebrow label repeated over every section, no identical icon+heading+text card grids, no fabricated dashboards or reviews.

**Key Characteristics:**
- Bone-paper broadsheet ground with per-section 1px vertical rails (hidden below 1080px)
- Warm charcoal consoles for every live-platform moment; ember hairline seam across their top edge
- Cargo Ember rationed to CTAs, live signals, and exactly three underlined payoff words
- Archivo worked at both ends of its width axis: extended (110–125%) for headings, condensed (80–92%) for figures and machine labels
- Four text weights only (450/500/600/700); 800 is Archivo-only and appears in exactly three places page-wide
- Mono only where values must align in a column — including the loss register's citation column
- Squared 6px controls with a 1px bottom ledge shadow and an arrow affordance
- Hairline-first depth; deep soft ambient shadows only under whole consoles and physical objects
- Isometric hairline dieline illustration system; real freight footage and label renders as imagery

## Colors

A warm, paper-and-charcoal neutral field where one ember accent does all the shouting and small cool accents identify technologies.

### Primary
- **Cargo Ember** (#BC3E10): the page's single loud voice. Primary CTAs, the live route on the map, the walkthrough gauge fill, active tab tints (`rgba(188,62,16,0.07–0.12)` washes with `0.24–0.45` borders), the comparison column's top rule, and the three `.h2-mark` payoff underlines. Hover state is **Ember Hover** (#DA4A10).
- **Ember family, by surface**: **Ember Dark** (#8E2D10) is ember-on-daylight text (explore links, "$10 smart label" captions — legible on bone/white); **Ember Bright** (#F9762F) is ember-on-charcoal (live routes, gauge fill, checkmarks, focus outlines, column-heading dashes); **Ember Soft** (#F8B181) is dim ember text on charcoal (mono chip labels, API strings, footer link hover); **Ember Arrow** (#F66923) draws arrow glyphs and the API `POST` verb on dark; **Ember Mid** (#E45011) appears only in the incident-stat data ramp.

### Named Rules
**The Two Worlds Rule.** Every surface is either bone paper or warm charcoal console. No mid-gray third theme, no cool-toned dark, no section that can't say which world it belongs to.

**The Ember Budget Rule.** Cargo Ember is a signal, not a paint. On any daylight surface it appears only on CTAs, active states, live data, and payoff underlines — roughly ≤10% of the surface. If a section feels orange, it's over budget.

**The Teal Chip Rule.** Verified Teal exists only as small verified-state chips and the XenAuth mark's accent. It never grows into a section wash, a heading color, or a large surface.

## Typography

**Display Font:** Archivo variable (wdth 62–125, wght 400–900; Helvetica Neue fallback)
**Body Font:** IBM Plex Sans variable (wdth 75–100, wght 400–700; Helvetica Neue fallback)
**Machine Font:** JetBrains Mono (ui-monospace fallback), weight 500 only

**Character:** One display face worked at both ends of its width axis. Headings stretch wider as they grow (125% on h1, 110% on h2, 100% on h3) while figures and machine labels *condense* to 80–92% — extended-against-condensed is the contrast the page runs on, and it costs nothing because both live in one variable file. Against that, a technical-documentation text face rather than a UI face.

Source Sans 3 was retired for a measured reason: its 486 x-height against Archivo's 526 made body copy render visibly paler than the heading above it, which the codebase had been compensating for with a `17px !important` patch and with 700-weight text everywhere. IBM Plex Sans (x-height 516, wider set width) removes the cause, so the weight ladder can mean something again. Its crossbarred capital I also disambiguates the serials this page is full of.

### The tracking curve
Optical tracking is a function of size, never a per-element nudge. Nine named steps, shipped as `--ls-*` tokens, crossing zero at ~17px:

`≥64px −0.028em` · `48–56px −0.022em` · `34–40px −0.016em` · `26–32px −0.010em` · `20–24px −0.004em` · `17–18px 0` · `15px +0.006em` · `13px +0.010em` · `11–12px (mono) +0.020em`

On charcoal the positive branch is halved — light-on-dark blooms optically.

### The weight ladder
Four text weights, full stop: **450** body, **500** micro and machine, **600** emphasis (never 700 inside a paragraph), **700** table and ledger values. **800 is Archivo-only** and appears in exactly three roles page-wide: the hero h1, the loss-register figures, and the footer wordmark.

`font-synthesis: none` is set globally — Plex stops at 700 and Archivo at 900, and without it a call past either end synthesizes a faux bold that reads subtly wrong.

### Hierarchy
Twelve roles, shipped as `.t-*` classes in `index.css`. Line-height always lives on the same rule that sets the size, because `calc(1em + 4px)` resolves against the element's own font-size and mis-computes silently if inherited.

- **Display** (Archivo 800, clamp(46px,6.2vw,92px), 0.98, wdth 125): the hero h1 and the footer wordmark only.
- **Headline** (Archivo 750, clamp(34px,4.4vw,56px), calc(1em+4px), wdth 110): every section h2, sentence case, left-aligned, max 22ch.
- **Title** (Archivo 700, clamp(24px,2.4vw,32px), calc(1em+4px), wdth 100): card and console h3s.
- **Subtitle** (Archivo 650, 20px, 1.15, wdth 100): h4.
- **Figure** (Archivo 800, clamp(52px,6vw,88px), 0.88, wdth 82, tabular-nums): the single largest figure on a surface — the stakes band's `$273,990` terminal. **Figure Headline** clamp(38px,4.6vw,62px) at wdth 80 sets the stakes band's three-up magnitudes; **Figure Major** clamp(28px,3vw,38px) at wdth 86 sets the comparison verdict and the connectivity numbers. Condensed 800 beside extended 800 is the page's primary type contrast.
- **Value** (Archivo 700, 17px, wdth 92, tabular-nums): comparison and ledger values only — never a sentence-length cell.
- **Label** (Archivo 600, 11.5px, +0.09em, uppercase, wdth 88): short machine strings. Replaces mono here, whose fixed advance makes these lines ~1.8× wider and reads loose rather than dense.
- **Lead** (Plex 450, 19px, 1.5): section decks. Retires the old `17px !important` patch.
- **Body** (Plex 450, 16px, 1.6) · **Small** (Plex 450, 13.5px, 1.5) · **Micro** (Plex 500, 12px, 1.4).
- **Machine** (JetBrains Mono 500, 12px, +0.02em, lining/tabular/slashed-zero): serials, temperatures, timestamps, the API panel, and the loss register's citation column.

### Two documented exemptions from the ramp
Both are deliberate, and both are the only exemptions that exist.

1. **Object-printed type.** Text printed *on a drawn physical object* — the hero label render's
   serial, FCC ID, `BLE + LTE-M` and `TAP`, and the machine chips inside the isometric dielines —
   scales with the object, not with the page's reading ramp. It is set in viewport-relative clamps
   as small as 6–11px because it is a photograph of printing, not something the reader is asked to
   read at reading distance. Treat these like the drop-shadow rule: the label render is an object,
   and objects follow their own physics.
2. **Third-party fidelity constants.** The AskDemo WhatsApp thread, shipped as `--wa-*` custom
   properties (see "Third-party UI fidelity"). Normalising them toward our roles makes the demo read
   as a knock-off.

Anything else off the ramp is drift, not a decision.

### Named Rules
**The Machine Voice Rule.** Mono is for values that must **align in a column** — serials, temperatures, timestamps, citations. Inline machine labels that do not need to align use condensed Archivo (the Label role) instead. If a human wrote the sentence, it is Plex. Never mix within one string.

**The One Scale Rule.** Every section h2 on the page is the same voice: 750 / clamp(34px,4.4vw,56px) / calc(1em+4px) at 110% width. Sections differentiate by content and surface, never by inventing a new heading size. (A ratio line-height cannot be correct at both ends of a fluid clamp; a constant increment can.)

**The Ember Phrase Rule.** (Owner-directed, Aug 2026 — this reinstates a device from earlier versions of the site.) Ember may colour the noun phrase that names what is being claimed — never the verb that makes the claim, and never more than a third of the heading's words. It appears on **exactly three headings page-wide**; a fourth turns a device into a tic, and once ember is in most headings it stops being emphasis and becomes the heading colour. Ship as `.h2-ember`, which resolves to Ember Dark (#8E2D10) on bone and Ember Bright (#F9762F) on charcoal — the same hue does not hold contrast on both grounds. Never on the hero h1: a two-tone extended-uppercase display line reads as gradient-adjacent, and the page's loudest voice stays one colour.

The three, and they are the three sections whose whole argument is *this fits what you already have* — a copy pattern doing real work, not decoration:
`#compare` "Read it against **what you run now**" · `#integrations` "Works with the tools **you already run**" · `#platform` "The signal lands where your team **already works**".

**The Three Marks Rule.** The `.h2-mark` device — a thick ember underline (0.075em thickness, 0.16em offset, `text-decoration-skip-ink: none`) on inherited ink — appears exactly three times: the hero payoff ("can't afford to lose."), "close that gap.", and "One platform." A payoff word is never a second text color, and no new section may add a fourth mark without retiring one.

## Layout

**Section order argues before it explains** — the category convention measured
off Samsara and Motive, then sharpened: hero, the spec ledger on the fold's
heels, then the PROBLEM (three published loss figures fused to the $10
comparison), then the demo, and only then the product tour. The visitor sees
what loss costs and tries the thing working before a single spec is asked of
them. The full run (owner-set, Aug 31): Hero → TrustBar → Stakes (the three
published loss figures, split out of the comparison and carrying the ember
heading phrase) → Products → AskDemo → Industries → HowItWorks → Video →
Integrations → Comparison (the table alone, headed "The $10 label, line by
line" in plain ink) → ProductsBanner → Clients → FAQ → CTA. Platform ("The
signal lands where your team already works") is unmounted for now at the
owner's request; the component stays on disk. Nav links mirror this sequence,
so the nav doubles as the argument's outline. Dark bands land at hero, the
product-overview banner, Integrations, and the closing CTA/footer — a cadence,
not an alternation.

**The FAQ is the objection station, and it sits immediately before the ask.**
Nine hairline rows in native `<details>`, so the accordion works with the
keyboard and with JavaScript off; the toggle is a mono `[+]` / `[−]` drawn in
CSS off `[open]`, which is this page's machine register rather than a borrowed
chevron icon. It does the one job no other section does: it names the catch a
team already running trackers goes looking for. Two rules hold it honest. The
questions are written in that buyer's voice, and no answer prints a figure that
is not either provable on this page or linked to a resolving source — the
coverage question in particular is where a network-scale number would
traditionally go, and it deliberately gets the mechanism instead. Its heading
takes `.section-heading` like every other section h2: a centred FAQ heading was
the one thing that broke the broadsheet's left measure.

A single-column broadsheet. Content sections run in a 1240px container with 32px gutters (`padding: 0 32px`); the hero and dark bands widen to 1300px, the trust ledger to 1320px, the footer to 1480px, and the comparison narrows to a 1016px reading measure. Vertical rhythm is `--section-space: clamp(96px,10vw,136px)` between sections, stepping to 80px below 760px and 64px below 560px.

Each `.section-shell` is framed by two 1px vertical rails (`rgba(20,17,13,0.1)`) pinned to its container edges — the broadsheet measure — hidden below 1080px. Full-bleed charcoal bands (ProductsBanner, footer) carry no rails.

Section headings are left-aligned (`.section-heading`, max 54rem): h2 capped at 22ch, lede at 56ch/17px, with 64px below the heading block (40px mobile). The hero is a `1.1fr 0.9fr` grid (copy left, label render right) over full-bleed freight footage with a warm charcoal two-axis scrim, the client marquee pinned to the fold's bottom edge. Consoles use asymmetric grids (`1.15fr 0.85fr` walkthrough, `0.85fr 1.15fr` platform, `1.05fr 0.95fr` banner).

Responsive sequence: 1080px — rails off, vertical stat grids to 2-up; 980px — nav links collapse to burger, hero and platform/API grids stack; 820px — two-column grids stack, footer to 2-up; 760px — section rhythm drops to 80px, the manifest table header hides and rows stack; 640px — the comparison sheet scrolls sideways under a 44px right-edge white fade (`linear-gradient(90deg,rgba(255,255,255,0) → #FFFFFF 82%)`) instead of clipping silently; 560px — 20px gutters, 64px rhythm; 440px — trust ledger to one column.

## Elevation & Depth

Hairline-first. Structure is drawn with 1px rules — `rgba(20,17,13,0.14)`/`0.08` on bone, `rgba(255,255,255,0.06–0.09)` on charcoal — and flat surface steps (bone → cloud wells → paper panels; night → night-raise → night-panel). Shadows exist in exactly three roles: a deep soft ambient under whole plates, a 1px ledge under squared buttons, and `drop-shadow` filters under drawn physical objects. Glow is reserved for live signals.

Dark consoles additionally announce themselves with an **ember seam**: a 1px top hairline in `linear-gradient(90deg, transparent, rgba(249,118,47,0.45), transparent)` spanning the middle 80% (CTA panel, footer, platform console).

### Shadow Vocabulary
- **Console ambient** (`box-shadow: 0 2px 6px rgba(20,17,13,0.08), 0 40px 80px -48px rgba(20,17,13,0.5)`): the walkthrough console, connectivity band, and integrations manifest resting on the bone ground.
- **Card ambient** (`box-shadow: 0 1px 2px rgba(20,17,13,0.03), 0 28px 56px -40px rgba(20,17,13,0.35)`): daylight paper panels (product dossiers; comparison frame at 0.3 alpha). Always paired with a 1px border.

### Named Rules
**The Hairline First Rule.** Reach for a 1px rule or a flat tonal step before any shadow or wash. A shadow is earned only by a whole console, a squared CTA's ledge, or a drawn physical object — never by a text block, list row, or inner tile.

## Shapes

Squared, machined, papery. Controls sit at 6px radius (`--radius-sm`) — equipment buttons, not pills — and every panel, console, card, and stage normalizes to 8px (`--radius-md`; `index.css` enforces this with `!important` on `.platform-grid`, `.cta-panel`, `.film-frame`, `.industry-stage`, `.connectivity-card`). The only 12px surfaces are the hero's physical label render and the video/industry frames' inner media; reg chips drop to 4px. Fully round shapes are reserved for signal dots, map markers, and the drawn phone hardware (40px shell / 32px screen — a device, not a UI surface).

The recurring silhouette is the **dieline**: isometric hairline wireframes (cartons, pallets, label plates, radio arcs) at 1.1–1.6px strokes in `rgba(255,255,255,0.16–0.45)` with ember or teal only on the label/tap elements; barcode tick marks and dashed aggregation links carry the same language. UI icons share one 1.8px stroke (Lucide geometry); the label render's NFC target is a 1.5px dashed square. Borders are ubiquitous: every raised surface has a 1px hairline before it has anything else.

## Icon construction

All drawn icons follow the measured Lucide-grid rules: 24px canvas, at least 1px optical
padding, exactly one stroke width per set (product marks and utility glyphs use **1.75px** —
the animejs.com value, between Lucide's 2 and Geist's 1.5). The 1.75 is **optical**: a glyph
rendered smaller than its canvas scales its declared stroke up so the drawn line still measures
~1.75px on screen (Lucide's absoluteStrokeWidth rule — e.g. 1.9 declared on the 24 grid at a
22px render, 2.2 at 16px). Round caps and round joins,
corner radii 2px on shapes ≥8px and 1px below, minimum 2px between sub-elements, straight
segments on the half-pixel grid, and at most three distinct elements at a 16px render.

Color: monochrome `currentColor`/`color`, with the product's single accent token allowed
only on the signal element (ember for XenTag's radio arcs, teal for XenAuth's tap arcs) —
never a second filled hue, never a filled blob mixing weights. The measurable line between
professional and cartoonish is exactly this: one stroke, one cap policy, fixed radii,
restrained element count.

Product identity: **the product is shown as the object itself wherever it can be shown at
size** — the white label mock with its real barcode and serial, the black NFC chip card.
Where it cannot, it is shown as a **drawn portrait of that object** — the same label, the
same tag — never as a metaphor or a generic category glyph. A product mark is a
scale-reduced object.

The two marks obey five rules, and obeying all five is what makes them read as a set:
**(1) One envelope, turned 90°** — both bodies are the same rounded rectangle at rx 2.5;
identical corner geometry does most of the family work. XenTag is landscape (a label lies
flat on a carton), XenAuth portrait (a tag is held up to be tapped). **(2) Two weights** —
contour 1.75px, interior detail 1.15px, a 1.52:1 ratio; this is what turns "a rounded rect
with lines in it" into "a label with something printed on it". **(3) One accent, one place**
— exactly one accent per mark, only on the signal element. **(4) One true quarter-arc**,
centred on the mark's own dot, never floating. **(5) Contact** — XenTag's dot sits ON the
label's right edge and the arc leaves it; XenAuth's arc arrives from outside and terminates
ON the tag's left edge. That inversion is the near-field story told geometrically.

On charcoal the strokes drop to 1.60/1.05 (`dark` prop): a light-on-dark stroke reads ~1.25x
heavier than the same stroke dark-on-light. The plate is 40px at radius 6px with the glyph at
26px — a 65% fill ratio, the measured product-tile figure; 55% is why the marks previously
looked marooned. **The plate stays neutral in both themes** — never tint it per product, or it
reads as a status colour. Colour lives only inside the glyph. Below 24px the detail band fuses
into a grey smear, so a compact variant thins it; never render either mark below 20px. Third-party tool marks (integrations hub) are the
real brand glyphs, monochrome white at ~17px in 36px hairline chips — letter monograms
only where no faithful mark exists (Geotab).

## The demo network panel (Ask-on-WhatsApp, left half)

The section's whole claim is that the manifest and the bot read **one live
state**, so that has to be visible rather than asserted.

**The map is real geography, drawn rather than photographed.** Natural Earth
1:110m coastlines and country borders (public domain), simplified with
Ramer-Douglas-Peucker and shipped as path data in `src/data/worldMap.js`. It
replaced a Web Mercator coverage screenshot that could show coverage but could
not show where anything *is* — the eight assets existed only as text.

The projection is doing real work. **Equirectangular means the viewBox is
literally degrees**, so `x = lng + 180` and `y = 90 - lat`: a pin's coordinates
*are* its screen position, and an asset can never drift from where it actually
is. A Mercator basemap image cannot make that promise without per-image
calibration, which is exactly why the photographic version shipped with no pins
at all. Every asset carries a real `coord` in `useFleetSim`.

The frame is cropped to 168°W–150°E, 60°N–12°S — where freight actually is.
Full-globe framing spends half the panel on empty Pacific. Styled as a tracking
console, not an atlas: water recedes, land sits one value step up, borders are
a hairline, and the only saturated ink on the surface is the assets. Each
pin carries a white halo, without which a dot on a coastline reads as part of
the coastline. Regenerate from source rather than hand-editing the paths.

**The map zooms, and the camera follows the conversation.** Geometry is 1:50m
(RDP eps 0.08°, finer than one screen pixel at the 8× zoom cap) and loads
lazily so it costs the initial bundle nothing. Geographic strokes are
`vector-effect: non-scaling-stroke` and pin radii divide by the zoom factor —
a hairline stays a hairline and a pin holds its screen size at any zoom, which
is what separates a tracking console from a scaled picture. Serials fade in at
k ≥ 2.4, exactly when the zoom makes them legible. Manual control is squared
equipment buttons (plus drag-pan when zoomed, double-click, and pinch as
ctrl+wheel); zoom flights interpolate k in log space so 1×→6× moves at a steady
perceived rate, and `prefers-reduced-motion` jumps instead of flying. When the
bot answers about assets the camera flies to frame exactly those pins; an
aggregate answer flies home. A caption bar under the frame carries the state
key and provenance.

**Em dashes are banned from rendered copy.** Place strings join with a middle
dot (`Rotterdam · Maasvlakte II`), asides become sentences or commas. The em
dash survives only in code comments and internal docs.

**The resolver answers from the dataset, or says why it cannot.** Three rules
govern it. Every answer reads `fleet` at answer time, so the thread, manifest
and map can never disagree. A list answers with **all** its matches — "anything
in transit?" returning one of four would be a wrong answer, not a short one, so
a four-match answer lights four pins. And anything outside the dataset gets a
real reply naming what is missing and what *can* be asked instead: pricing,
routing a human, acting on an asset, history, forecasting, fleet edits and
non-XenTag telemetry each have their own honest response, never a guess.

Two traps worth recording. `keys` on an asset are for **identity only** —
serial fragments, commodity, a distinctive place. A condition word in there
(`temp`, `breach`, `yard`, `quiet`) or a region hijacks every condition
question containing it, because a named asset is checked before conditions.
And an **action verb outranks a serial**: "reroute XT-48192" is a request to
*do* something, so answering it with a location would dodge the question.

**The comparison header names both contenders at full weight** — Archivo 700/17
with the XenTag mark against a 500-weight incumbent, each with a mono price
sub — because the reader must never work out whose column is whose. The paper
emphasis column is hairline-edged (`rgba(20,17,13,.1)`) so it reads as mounted
rather than misaligned, and its offsets track the grid at every breakpoint.
The section's one authored motion: rows fade up 8px behind a 25ms stagger, the
paper settles first, and the verdict's $10 counts up once with fixed digits.

**The link is stated in three places, one serial.** When the thread answers
about an asset: its pin takes a focus ring on the map, its row lights in the
manifest, a focus bar names it with its live readings (`THREAD IS SHOWING
XT-48219 · TEMP BREACH · +6.4 °C — above the +4 °C limit · Battery 84% ·
Pinged 2 minutes ago`), and the phone header changes from "online" to
"showing XT-48219". Because every one of those reads the same projected state
at answer time, the panel and the thread can never disagree.

**Status is a word, not a colour.** Every row and the focus bar carry a state
chip — In transit / Delivered / Temp breach / Left geofence / No signal — at
10px mono uppercase on a 10–14% tint of its own hue. Colour alone is not a
status: it fails a colour-blind reader and it fails in a screenshot.

**No voice affordance.** The composer's send control is a send control in every
state; it is disabled until there is something to send. A microphone implies a
capability this demo does not have.

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do:
- **Do** frame every bone-ground section with the `.section-shell` rails and keep dark bands rail-free — the contrast between measured paper and full-bleed plate is the layout's voice.
- **Do** hold every section h2 to the One Scale (800 / clamp(34px,4.4vw,56px) / 1.04) and every machine value to JetBrains Mono tabular-nums.
- **Do** keep controls squared at 6px with the 1px ledge shadow and an arrow affordance; panels normalize to 8px.
- **Do** draw new illustrations in the isometric hairline-dieline language (1.1–1.6px wireframes, ember/teal only on the label and tap elements).
- **Do** preserve the honest states: capture success only after a real 2xx, the failed state's `mailto:` fallback, `prefers-reduced-motion` kills (global animation kill, static gauge, unmasked scrollable marquee), and the marquee's hover/touch-hold pause.
- **Do** signal "live" with green dots + glow and "route/active" with ember — and use warm charcoal (#151210 family) for any new dark surface, never cool gray.

### Don't:
- **Don't** add a fourth `.h2-mark`, a fourth ember heading phrase, or gradient text anywhere. (A partial-ember phrase inside a heading is now allowed — see The Ember Phrase Rule — but gradient text never is.)
- **Don't** exceed the Ember Budget — no ember section washes, tinted backgrounds beyond the ~0.07–0.12 active tints, or decorative orange.
- **Don't** reintroduce generic-SaaS furniture: uppercase eyebrow labels over every section, identical icon+heading+text grids, glassmorphism beyond the nav's functional blur, emoji-as-icon.
- **Don't** fabricate dashboards, reviews, or success UI — the platform section shows the production screenshot, captioned as such.
- **Don't** put box-shadows on inner tiles, rows, or drawn objects (objects get `drop-shadow` filters), and don't glow anything that isn't a live signal.
- **Don't** clip wide content silently — the comparison's right-edge fade is the pattern for horizontal overflow on narrow screens.
